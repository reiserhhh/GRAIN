## 1. Why a qualified output is not a qualified key code

HM132 identifies a historical-key intervention as the dominant carrier of a
behavior/owner/queried-person output interaction. It does not identify which
part of that key is doing the work. A content-independent ownership increment
can produce exactly this interaction by selecting behavior-dependent values.

For a one-head two-position example with signs b,o,p, choose

    K1=o*kappa, K2=-o*kappa; Q=p*q;
    V1=b*c, V2=-b*c.

Then the attention read is exactly

    A(b,o,p)=b*c*tanh(o*p*q*kappa)=b*o*p*c*tanh(q*kappa).

There is no bo key component; the bop output is nonzero nevertheless. This is
the ordinary two-logit softmax identity. A large K intervention effect cannot
tell whether K stores a unique behavioral/personality code. Output interactions
and representation interactions are distinct objects.

This resolves a concrete reasoning risk: replacing "K is necessary for
qualification" with "personality resides in K" confuses selection and valuation.
The same mistake can occur if a high-accuracy role probe is interpreted as an
explanation of the behavioral inference itself.

## 2. Selective registered-key operations

At fixed absolute positions, each actual four-corner family has the exact
finite decomposition

    K(b,o)=K0+b*Kb+o*Ko+b*o*Kbo.

The four sectors are character projections of (Z/2Z)^2. They are complete on
this finite support, not learned factors or globally pure semantic coordinates.
The labels "role" and "joint" specify their change under designed edits only.
Ko is independent of the b sign within one family, but can differ across families.
K0/Kb themselves can contain behavioral information. Zero Kbo does NOT make
the whole key behavior-blind.

HM133 independently admits Ko and Kbo in native suffix attention at all blocks.
Actual V and prefix computation are unchanged, and Q evolves recursively. Thus
the experiment measures the action of specified finite key transformations,
not the fraction of human personality in a neuron or a fixed attention head.
Padding, event context and length can influence the projections. Registering
declaration positions removes one address confound, not every semantic alternative.

Output inclusion/exclusion is also exact on the four intervention settings:

    Y11=Y00+R+J+I,
    R=Y10-Y00, J=Y01-Y00, I=Y11-Y10-Y01+Y00.

R/J may oppose; I is the joint action of permitting the two components, not a
third independent physical carrier. Signed projection can be negative or >1.
No derivative linearity or independent psychological information is assumed.

## 3. Construction consequences for GRAIN

The existing measurement chain contains different functions:

    qualified evidence history
      -> registered vector carriers
      -> person/target-conditioned selection and valuation
      -> an observation in a specified unit
      -> declared author-evidence composition and reference comparison.

These stages should not be replaced by a single fitted embedding-to-answer
map. If common role increments suffice in HM133, actor indexing is a reusable
front-end operation, and the next unknown concerns behavioral valuation and
target-conditioned composition. If content-conditioned increments contribute,
the observation interface must preserve them instead of deleting them as noise.
Neither outcome requires first increasing PANDORA classification accuracy.

For a fixed observation table N_q(b,o,p), a DECLARED linear evidence account is

    S_q(P,p)=sum_(b,o) P(b,o)*N_q(b,o,p).

For same-marginal positive evidence laws P+/-=(1+/-eta*b*o)/4,

    S_q(P+,p)-S_q(P-,p)=2*eta*(beta_bo,q+p*beta_bop,q).

The inclusion/exclusion account applies to each term. Equal content counts
therefore do not force equal qualified-author observations. This finite
integration is not a theorem that scoring a concatenated natural conversation
equals averaging its events: HM102/HM110/HM111 show composition matters.
Retain both event law and chosen composition operation, conditions, chronology,
opportunities and state. No real evidence is relabeled after results.
