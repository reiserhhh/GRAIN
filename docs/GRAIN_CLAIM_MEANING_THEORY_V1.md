## 2. An observation involution with an explicit scope

Let p_j(u) say that a complete quoted first-person behavior describes the named
author's usual behavior. Its logical denial is not p_j(u). It is NOT a claim
that the person usually performs an opposite behavior. Positive and negative
IPIP poles are therefore not substituted for this operation.

For fixed evidence E and role/display design r, let pi_plus(E,r,j) and
pi_minus(E,r,j) be the separately observed three-category response laws. The
categories are contradiction, support, and insufficient evidence about THE
CLAIM. Let S exchange the first two categories and retain the third.

    S(pi0,pi1,pi2) = (pi1,pi0,pi2), S^2 = identity.

A declared content-following ideal is pi_minus=S(pi_plus). Actual observations
need not satisfy it. Different wording, affirmative/negative processing, model
response habits and semantic uncertainty can violate this ideal. The experiment
records that failure rather than enforcing it or declaring every deviation noise.

Use d=log(pi1/pi0), a=log((pi0+pi1)/pi2). The ideal acts as (d,a)->(-d,a).
The additive-logit gauge disappears in these contrasts; category mass in the
full vocabulary is separately retained. Neither d nor a is human truth.
