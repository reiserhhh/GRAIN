## Mathematics and Its Status

For finite evidence values f and n, and all strictly positive normalized evidence
laws p and q, equality of the weak order of p.f and q.f with that of p.n and q.n
requires n = a*f + b, a > 0, when f is nonconstant. The constant case must preserve
all ties. This is a basic finite affine-representation result, not new mathematics.

Proof: differences of interior probability laws span the zero-sum subspace. Equal
weak orders require equal kernels there, hence positive proportional functionals;
the orthogonal complement of that subspace is the constant vector. The converse
follows by substitution. This statement assumes the complete simplex; it must not
be applied without modification to fixed-margin author laws.

Event-wise increasing correspondence is weaker. For f=(0,1,2), n=(0,1,4), all
events have the same order, but p=(.4,.2,.4) and q=(.1,.8,.1) have equal f means
and unequal n means. This is a classical illustration, not a discovered mechanism.

For fixed content/owner marginals use only the registered joint laws
p+=(.375,.125,.125,.375), p-=(.125,.375,.375,.125). Their contrast is I/4, where
I=f0-f1-f2+f3. This one-dimensional family may preserve order even when complete-
simplex equivalence fails. Report the two questions separately.

Primary literature context: Luce & Tukey (1964), DOI
10.1016/0022-2496(64)90015-X (publisher abstract read; retrieved full text blocked),
and Principi, Wakker & Wang (2025), DOI 10.3982/TE5799, sections 1-2 and Theorem 1
read at https://personal.eur.nl/wakker/pdfspubld/antimon.pdf. The latter identifies
critical additivity tests in its decision-theoretic domain. We borrow the practice
of testing defining operations, not its behavioral axioms or conclusions about
human utility. No OpenAI/math PDE/geometry theorem is being applied in this packet.
