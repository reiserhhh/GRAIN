# From selecting personal evidence to interpreting its diagnostic value

2026-10-11. Theory-development note; HM134 finite execution is now verified.
No claim of a new Bayes theorem, human personality validation or global closure.

## What changed after HM133

The native key operation in HM133 mostly survives a source-common role key
increment. It selects whose record is accessed, not a unique personality vector.
A score fitted more accurately to that selection program would not explain why
one selected action implies extraversion while another implies reassurance seeking.
The target step is a conditional behavioral observation law, not identity decoding.

Keep three mathematically distinct maps:

    selected record e = (actor, action, context, modality, event, history)
    observer operation y_q = F_q(e)
    measurement likelihood ell_q(e | theta, design)

Actual interventions on F_q identify operations of a frozen observer on their
specified support. They do not by themselves determine ell_q for people.
GRAIN's evidence-unit and author-account work can retain the relevant relations
without pretending the observer output is a psychological truth label.

## Conditional observation is not deleting context

If an author's disposition theta can affect both context selection z and action a,

    p(theta|a,z) proportional to ell(a|theta,z) s(z|theta) p(theta).

The action likelihood ell is one source of information, the selection law s another.
When assignment is randomized independently of theta, s is constant in theta.
If the exact act is imposed independently as well, ell can also be constant;
the record then contains no information about theta under that assumed model.
If the person chooses a setting or can comply/decline, those observations can
still be diagnostic. We must retain them rather than residualize all conditions.

The example is a generative assumption, not a rule that any assigned speech has
zero information. Unknown constraint strength, imperfect compliance, narration
choices and other recorded acts alter the likelihood. Real semantics must decide
which observation was made before a numerical likelihood can be justified.

## Why more accurate scores cannot solve identification alone

Suppose an unlabeled population contains theta+ and theta- with equal prior.
Model1 has P(a=1|theta+)=.8 and P(a=1|theta-)=.2. Model2 has both probabilities .5.
Both produce P(a=1)=.5. Their predicted observable marginal can be equally exact,
but observing a=1 gives P(theta+|a)=.8 in Model1 and .5 in Model2. Large sample
size removes error in the marginal; it does not identify the unobserved disposition
mechanism. More conditions, external anchors or justified structural assumptions
are needed. This is a standard mixture-identification counterexample.

Similarly, different K/V operations may coincide at ordinary text endpoints.
Crossed interventions can distinguish them even when endpoint precision cannot.
HM132/HM133 show the utility of this distinction; their finite programs do not
imply uniqueness of a natural-language or psychological mechanism.

## The next empirical contribution sought

HM134 keeps an executed action fixed and supplies a later causal clarification.
It measures cause-only level, behavior-by-cause contrast, unacting-person effects
and unchanged action facts, then crosses cause K/V. This separates a lexical
behavior association, generic evaluation and a conditional judgment operation.
None is assumed absent; negative or opposing effects remain part of the account.
The contribution would be an observed operation on newly authored materials, not
the already-known psychological statement that causes can change trait inference.

An actionable positive result requires updating the evidence/observation interface:
represent cause/choice separately from behavior identity, preserve the full
multi-target response field, and keep the author's condition-choice information.
It does not require maximizing correlation with a teacher or discovering a new
linear trait axis. If only levels change, the observer supports that narrower
operation, and no trait-specific Bayesian mechanism should be inferred from it.

## HM134 result and changed construction decision

All196 batches/2744 endpoints completed in899.006532 modelseconds, without fit
or precision search. Nine native controls are exact; alternate arithmetic error
3.55e-15. In score units the actor's behavior-by-cause RMS is .037902/.068734;
the non-actor's is .051490/.043897. Unchanged action-fact scores also move by
up to .325652/.300991 for A. Conditional operation exists, but it is not a pure
change in the actor's trait diagnosticity with factual recognition held fixed.

The cause action is not merely a scalar offset. Its K/V signed projections onto
the actor's interaction field are [-.050876,.949748,.101128] and
[-.199222,.363665,.835557] in the two banks. These are algebraic projections,
not information percentages. Unlike the common role-key operation in HM133,
cause effects need substantial value/joint operation, with wording and unit
dependence. These task-dependent operations do not support a universal
K=identity/V=personality division of labor.

Do not tune this observer to repair the control or fit a substitute to its
outputs. First distinguish the recognized event e, the observer F and the
diagnostic likelihood ell. Inventory existing VM01/NAV/F03 evidence laws before
designing another finite intervention; preserve context-choice information.
The finding advances operation separation, not human prediction accuracy or a
new Bayes theorem. Parent report: GRAIN_CAUSAL_VALUATION_MECHANISM_REPORT_20261011_ZH.md.
