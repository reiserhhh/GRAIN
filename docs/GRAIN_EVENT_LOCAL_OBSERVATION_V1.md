# Event-local and shared role observation for GRAIN

2026-10-08. Executed finite packets HM-04 and HM-05 extend HM-03.
The original research scope and psychological linkage are not closed by them.

## Bound observation unit

An observation has an addressed action, object, subject, polarity, report mode,
and externally supplied event/version identity. Syntax projects roles from raw
text; source-fitted vector functions read k,p,o,a. Evaluation labels never enter
the observation functions. Explicit subjects override shared ones. Unresolved
scope and uncovered records remain explicit, not neutral author observations.

For a target concerning reported actual action:

\[
c_Q(e)=p_e g(k_Q(e))g(o_e)g(a_e),\qquad
g(t)=\operatorname{clip}((1+t)/2,0,1).
\]

Relevance uses the frozen typed action-object kernel from HM-03. Other roles are
read locally, not taken from the whole mixed document. This gate is specific to
the stated observation purpose; it is not a universal rule to discard imagined
expression from every psychological measurement.

Each supplied event identity contributes once. Alias variability is reported;
the first supplied observation is retained. New events may update the author.
Different reports about a real event are not automatically identified from equal
words. Natural event identity inference is a separate interface problem.

Two units are explicitly different:

\[
U_Q(u)=\frac1{|E_u|}\sum_{e\in E_u}c_Q(e),\qquad
S_Q(u)=\frac1{|E_{u,Q}^{obs}|}\sum_{e\in E_{u,Q}^{obs}}c_Q(e).
\]

`U` is a contribution bookkeeping unit. `S` exists only with eligible observed
events and complete required scope. No such evidence gives `null`, not a neutral
person. A contribution zero may instead mean an unrelated event or a hypothetical
action relative to this particular target.

## Joint moments and compression

On ideal +/-1 role coordinates, the contribution mean is the sum of the eight
polarity-containing square-free moments, divided by eight. Separate marginal
means are insufficient: the same per-coordinate distributions can correspond
to opposite own-author evidence. Whole-event permutation preserves this binding;
independent column permutations do not. For continuous readouts the identity
applies after clipping the three gate coordinates, but does not replace hard
eligibility, event history, or target-specific measurement design.

This is a standard finite polynomial identity, not an original group-theoretic
personality theorem. It explains why more observations cannot restore a joint
relationship already discarded by marginal compression.

## Common role map and target-specific units

Within one frozen reader and aligned declared role units, compile each affine
source readout as `sigma_Q(h) = h W_Q + b_Q`. Set, before evaluation:

\[
\bar\sigma=(\sigma_1+\sigma_2)/2,\quad d=(\sigma_1-\sigma_2)/2.
\]

Common +/- difference reconstructs the native observations exactly. The choice
of common reference is not unique, and calibration need not transport for free.
HM-05 keeps target relevance native and tests cross-target/common p/o/a. Every
mixture role direction survives, but contribution amplitude is less accurate.

For mapped roles `v=(p,g(o),g(a))`, the native-common score difference expands
exactly into seven nonempty-subset product terms. These are overlapping algebraic
terms, not independent causal or psychological factors. Sharing role parameters
and averaging finished author scores are also different operations.
