## Observation object

A reader score depends on the behaviour described, its subject, factual mode,
outside appraisal and target query. Appraisal belongs to the named outside
observer, not automatically to the author. These roles remain in the ledger.

For one behaviour level b and three appraisal levels l, the exact finite law is

```text
y[b,l] = y[b,none] + p_l P_b + v_l E_b
p_none=0, p_positive=p_negative=1
v_none=0, v_positive=1, v_negative=-1
P_b=(y[b,positive]+y[b,negative])/2-y[b,none]
E_b=(y[b,positive]-y[b,negative])/2.
```

This is an observed factorial decomposition, not an extrapolating predictor.
High/low behaviour differences give D_l. Comparing full content-by-query matrices:

```text
D_l=gamma_l D_none+R_l
gamma_l=<D_none,D_l>_F/||D_none||_F^2
<D_none,R_l>_F=0.
```

The RMS of mean-b positive-minus-negative appraisal contrasts is 29.60%-36.02%
of the corresponding no-appraisal behavioural contrast RMS across six groups.
Negative-appraisal gamma ranges .6919-.7855; orthogonal remainder accounts for
7.20%-14.87% of its matrix squared energy. Positive appraisals mostly preserve
the reference matrix shape. These quantities are reader response geometry,
not human trait variance or accuracy, and are signed before summary.

## Mechanism implications

An observation law needs both a content association and relational qualification.
An unchanged action can be read differently when an outside observer appraises it.
Do not presume that the appraisal channel is useless, psychological truth, or
entirely one common evaluative factor. No-appraisal versus present changes length;
the shared presence channel cannot be identified solely as appraisal valence.

Future-appraisal additions cannot causally alter an earlier hidden prefix in the
autoregressive graph. The observed MPS prefix discrepancy4.387e-5 is reported,
not mistaken for backward influence and not used to justify a precision sweep.
