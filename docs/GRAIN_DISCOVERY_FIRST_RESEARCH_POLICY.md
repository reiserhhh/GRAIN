# GRAIN research rule: discovery first

2026-09-07. Explicit user instruction, shared by Codex, Claude, and future agents.

## Mainline

Discover how real author text/context becomes vector evidence and personality
inference. Use GRAIN's group-referenced author structure to organize and compare
those pathways. The purpose is a measurement/inference methodology, not generic
text diagnostics or a finished clinical scale.

## Required working order

1. Find a usable relationship, effect, or local mechanism.
2. Develop and connect it to the next mainline link.
3. Strengthen the result with focused verification after something exists to study.

A usable association or local effect is a success at that level. Perfect coding
invariance, universal transport, exhaustive controls, or a publication-ready theory
are not entrance gates for discovery. Retain earlier useful findings when adding
new qualifications. Do not replace discovery with an ever-growing defense program.

Before any side experiment, name the mainline decision it can change and bound
its effort. Defer secondary objections to a follow-up ledger. If a diagnostic
does not change the next real-author experiment, do not let it become the default
continuation. Do not automatically continue P1U or expand the synthetic P1 series.

## Reporting and minimum correctness

Lead with the finding, effect size, and what it enables. Describe the observed
population and method directly. Avoid repetitive deficit-first wording such as
"cannot show", "not yet proven", or "not stable enough" as the main conclusion.
Exploratory findings need not be promoted to confirmation to count as progress.

Keep a small correctness floor: protect private data, preserve authorized uses,
verify author/label joins, avoid train/test leakage, retain negative conditions,
and correct arithmetic that changes the finding. Do not invent positive effects
or significance. Minor uncertainty belongs in concise context, not an indefinite
block on the next experiment. Never silently open a sealed lockbox.
