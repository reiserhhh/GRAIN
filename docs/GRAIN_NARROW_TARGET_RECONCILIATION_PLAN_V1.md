## Reference averaging question

For local charts f_r(x)=b_r + w_r'(x-z_r), weights pi_r sum to one. The historical
constructor averages each array separately:

    f_mean_anchor(x) = mean(b) + mean(w)' (x-mean(z)).

Arithmetic averaging of whole functions instead gives

    f_chart_mean(x) = mean(b-w'z) + mean(w)'x
                    = f_mean_anchor(x) - kappa,
    kappa = mean(w'z) - mean(w)'mean(z).

Use the concatenated P/Q coordinate, retaining the P and Q terms separately.
These conventions have identical derivatives and endpoint differences. They
differ by a query-specific constant. Mean-anchoring has the useful property
mean_r[f_mean_anchor(z_r)]=mean_r b_r. Therefore an offset is not by itself a bug
or a reason to replace the historical model. Neither convention is automatically
the frozen nonlinear model at the mean state. Mean-anchoring, averaging charts
and evaluating the nonlinear model at a mean state are three different objects.

Quantify kappa for all 25 narrow functions, verify the identities on source and
held cached states, verify that within-query external correlations are unchanged,
and record the required anchor convention in every function card. No clipping,
sign change, threshold, reference selection or selection between these conventions
using human scores. Old M/C/Q covariance results remain intact under a constant.

Mathematical inheritance: elementary affine expectation/covariance, and the
explicit-baseline viewpoint of Sundararajan, Taly & Yan (2017), *Axiomatic
Attribution for Deep Networks*, https://proceedings.mlr.press/v70/sundararajan17a.html.
The current partial-path averaged operator is not exact integrated gradients for
every author's full network; this source was already cited by M31, not new work.
