reader's relative event allocation are different objects. A source conditional
law is not a neural attention distribution. Closing an encoding edge does not
make source observations statistically independent. Having a common reader

## A conditional composition contract

Partition a current-query attention law into X, Z, outside. Keep its evidence
probability m=P(X union Z), its two within-bank conditional laws and its outside
weights. Replace only P(X|X union Z) by1/2. Equivalently

\[
A'_X=\tfrac m2 A_X/m_X,\quad
A'_Z=\tfrac m2 A_Z/m_Z,\quad A'_{out}=A_{out}.
\]

For value means fX,fZ, the local change is exactly

\[
O-O'=(m_X-m/2)(f_X-f_Z).
\]

This uses probability disintegration on a finite partition. It is not a new
theorem, subtraction of psychological noise or a claim about human event
frequency. Both banks stay visible. Relative token selection INSIDE a bank and
the combined evidence probability remain conditional on the actual query.

Across decoder layers, queries and continuation carriers adapt to the modified
output. Thus the final change is an intervention's TOTAL recursive effect,
not a sum of local terms or an isolated unique causal pathway. Outside weights
are unchanged locally at the CURRENT query; they need not equal those in the
unmodified network. No general gauge invariance is asserted.
